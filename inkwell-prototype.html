The Last Train Home

By the author·June 18, 2026

The station at Maranhalli closed in 1991, but my father kept its timetable folded in his shirt pocket until the day he died. He could recite it the way other men recited prayer — the 6:40 to the city, the 9:15 that only ran on Sundays, the last train home at a quarter past eleven.

I used to think it was nostalgia. I understand now that it was something closer to faith.

The platform is still there, if you know where the road forks past the dry well. The rails were lifted decades ago, but the gravel keeps their shape, two pale lines running off into the tall grass like the memory of a sentence you can almost finish.

He went there every evening. He would sit on the broken bench, take out the timetable, and check his watch against a train that had not arrived in thirty years. Neighbours called it grief. I called it stubbornness. Both of us were wrong.

On the last evening, I followed him. I sat down beside him without a word, and at exactly a quarter past eleven, I heard it — faint, impossible, the long exhale of a train slowing into a station that no longer existed. He smiled, folded the timetable, and stood as if to board.

“You always wait for the things you love,” he said. “Even after they stop coming.”
